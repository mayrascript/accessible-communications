# Accessible Communications

Apply the guidance for the channel from the first draft. For an everyday email, improve the text without adding an unnecessary accessibility section. When planning, auditing, or coordinating an event, consult the [event checklist](references/events.md).

Draft audience-facing copy in Spanish unless the user requests another language or names a different audience language. Keep official names, titles, and quotations as given. Reply to the user in the language appropriate to the conversation.

## Before sharing a draft

- **Text first:** Include the purpose, action, date and time (with time zone if relevant), location or link, and written contact channel as appropriate. Do not rely solely on audio, a QR code, an image, a video, or a call for instructions. If the only contact option is voice, offer a written equivalent where possible. Use descriptive links or readable URLs as the channel allows.
- **Language:** Respect how people identify themselves. In Spanish, follow their preference for terms such as “persona Sorda” or “persona hipoacúsica.” Say “Lengua de Señas” and name the agreed sign language; use “Lengua de Señas Colombiana (LSC)” only when applicable. Do not infer a preference from nationality or assume written Spanish is a universal substitute. Prefer clear terms such as “ponente,” “presentación,” and “convocatoria de propuestas” when natural in Spanish copy.
- **Graphics and documents:** Add concise, meaningful alt text where supported. Otherwise put essential information from the graphic in the copy. Use a clear hierarchy and readable contrast. Do not convey status by color alone. In documents and schedules, use selectable text, meaningful headings, and a logical reading order. Do not claim to have tested contrast or assistive reading unless you did.
- **Audio and video:** Provide a written equivalent and high-contrast captions. Have a person review captions for names, technical terms, and omissions before publication. A transcript or automated captions do not replace interpretation when it is required.
- **Announcements and registration:** Announce only confirmed support. Specify the language, covered sessions or rooms, timing, and format when known. Avoid absolute claims such as “100 % inclusivo.” Include a written contact channel and an optional question about accommodations such as interpretation or live captions; do not ask for a diagnosis or certificate. Share accommodation needs only with those coordinating support. Do not promise pending support or disclose personal information.

Spanish copy example: “¿Necesitas algún ajuste para participar? Escríbenos a [canal de contacto].”

## Check before delivery

- Are the purpose, action, and key details available in text?
- Are advertised accommodations confirmed, with pending items not presented as available?
- Are material checks not performed, such as captions or reading order, marked as pending verification?

## Scope

This skill does not authorize sending, publishing, hiring, changing existing events, or disclosing data. Keep to task scope and existing approvals. Do not transfer a person's or event's individual preferences to another context or present this guidance as a certification or universal legal rule.
