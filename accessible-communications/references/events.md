# Event Accessibility Checklist

1. **4–8 weeks before the event:** Plan funding, identify an owner, and arrange room-by-room coverage for talks, workshops, and networking. Agree on language and accommodations directly with the participant without making them responsible for logistics. Verify professional interpreters' experience with technical material. For interpreted sessions longer than 30 minutes, the source guide recommends two interpreters per room who alternate every 20–30 minutes.
2. **1–2 weeks before the event:** Coordinate with speakers and production. Share the schedule, slides, speaker notes, and glossary 48–72 hours in advance. Rehearse positions, neutral backgrounds, front lighting, microphones, contrast, and sightlines between speaker, interpreter, and screen from reserved seats. For remote audiences, confirm attendees can see the interpreter and turn on captions without the host's help. Plan visual alerts and an evacuation procedure with the venue.
3. **During the event:** Use microphones for audience questions or repeat the question before answering. Speak directly to the participant, allow time for interpretation, describe what you point to, and respect interpreter handovers and breaks. If captions fail, activate the backup, announce the issue in writing, and verify access or pause the affected session. Do not shift the fallback to the participant's phone or replace a professional interpreter with volunteers or AI avatars.
4. **After the event:** Review captions and materials before publishing, and provide a written feedback channel. Mark open checks as pending verification with an owner and date when known. Separate completed checks from recommendations.
